People argue endlessly about whether a dish is ``authentic'', but the word is rarely defined. Network studies of recipes have measured authenticity from ingredient lists alone. We test whether that measure agrees with the judgments of the people who cook the food, by comparing ingredient networks of 26,727 online recipes with the language of their reviews. We also separate two ideas that are easy to confuse: using a cuisine's typical ingredients, and combining them in its typical way. Among recipes with authenticity-related review language, endorsements are more frequent when ingredients form characteristic pairings. The association is modest and suggests that ingredient combinations capture one aspect of how this community describes authenticity.
