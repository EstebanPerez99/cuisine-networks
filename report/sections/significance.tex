People argue endlessly about whether a dish is ``authentic'', but the word is rarely defined. Network studies of recipes have measured authenticity from ingredient lists alone. We test whether that measure agrees with the judgments of the people who cook the food, by comparing ingredient networks of 26,727 online recipes with the language of their reviews. We also separate two ideas that are easy to confuse: using a cuisine's typical ingredients, and combining them in its typical way. We find that home cooks call a recipe authentic not because it lists typical single ingredients, but because it pairs ingredients in the cuisine's characteristic combinations.
