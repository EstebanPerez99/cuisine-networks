An online recipe can carry a cuisine label, an ingredient list, and comments from people who made it. These describe different aspects of the dish. A cuisine-specific ingredient combination can be measured from the recipe collection; whether a reviewer calls a recipe ``authentic'' is a judgment expressed in words. We ask whether the two are related in the Food.com community.

Ahn and colleagues measured the relative prevalence of ingredients and pairs across regional cuisines and called this quantity ``authenticity'' \cite{ahn2011flavor}. Here we use the same basic contrast but call it \emph{ingredient distinctiveness}, reserving ``authentic'' for the language in reviews. We start with a recipe--ingredient bipartite network for nine Food.com cuisine groups. We then ask two different questions: how much do ingredient lists separate the cuisine tags, and which pairs distinguish one tag from the others? Randomized networks help us tell the effect of frequently used individual ingredients from the effect of putting ingredients together. Finally, we give each recipe a pair score and an ingredient-only baseline for the planned review comparison.
