\subsection*{Review language and perceived authenticity}
We adapted the two-stage screening framework of Le et al.\ \cite{le2022authenticity} to detect home-cooking authenticity endorsements without relying on raw keyword counts.

\textbf{Word2Vec vocabulary screen.} We trained a skip-gram Word2Vec model \cite{mikolov2013word2vec} (100 dimensions, window 5, minimum count 5, 5 negative samples, 5 epochs) on all 132,744 cleaned reviews (608,802 sentences; 13,380 tokens). Querying the top-10 nearest neighbors of Le et al.'s 88 authenticity seeds and six recipe-attribute groups (dish, ingredient, preparation, sensory, origin, reference), followed by manual curation, added 10 authenticity terms (e.g., \emph{recreate}, \emph{authentically}, \emph{copycat}, \emph{authenic}) and 187 attribute terms (e.g., \emph{basmati}, \emph{chapati}, \emph{marination}, \emph{grandma}). Requiring at least one sentence to contain both an authenticity term and a recipe attribute anchored broad words (\emph{real}, \emph{true}) to food rather than conversational filler, narrowing the corpus 18-fold to 7,281 candidate reviews (5.48\%) across 4,295 recipes.

\textbf{Clause-level KWIC rules.} Each candidate review was split into clauses on \texttt{. ! ? ;} and \emph{but}, and evaluated with four keyword-in-context (KWIC) rules mapping to the \emph{Authenticity of the Other} cues of Le et al.\ \cite{le2022authenticity} plus home-cooking edits:
(1)~\emph{Conformity} (endorse): \emph{authentic*}, \emph{traditional*}, \emph{genuine}, \emph{real thing}, or \emph{real deal};
(2)~\emph{Deviation} (block): negation (\emph{not}, \emph{never}, \emph{no}, \emph{hardly}, \emph{lacks}, \emph{without}, \emph{far from}, \emph{-n't}) within seven words before the cue;
(3)~\emph{Connection} (endorse): non-negated resemblance (\emph{just like}, \emph{exactly like}, \emph{reminds me of}, \emph{tastes like/close to the original});
(4)~\emph{Recipe edits} (block): modification phrasing in the same clause (\emph{more authentic}, \emph{instead of}, \emph{substitute*}, \emph{would/could/should}, \emph{next time}, \emph{to make it more}).
A review is endorsed if Rule~1 fires unblocked by Rules~2 and~4, or if Rule~3 fires. Rules were validated against 100 hand-labeled candidate reviews (22 endorsements).

\textbf{Aggregation, shuffle nulls, and embedding check.} A recipe is endorsed if at least one screened review is endorsed (4,275 of 4,295 screened recipes had valid pairing scores). Associations with each network score were measured by Spearman's $\rho$ and tested against 2,000 within-cuisine shuffles of the endorsement labels to obtain a null $z$-score and two-sided empirical $p$-value, with a volume-controlled check restricted to the 3,214 recipes having exactly one screened review. As a rule-free check, we encoded all 7,281 candidate reviews with \texttt{gte-small} (384 dimensions), fit a balanced logistic regression on a separate 100-review training split (5-fold CV cutoff capping false-positive rate at 10\%), and repeated the 2,000-shuffle test on both binary recipe endorsements and maximum continuous recipe scores.
