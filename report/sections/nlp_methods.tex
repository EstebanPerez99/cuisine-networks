\subsection*{Review language and perceived authenticity}
We adapted the two-stage framework of Le et al.\ \cite{le2022authenticity} to identify reviews that endorse a recipe's authenticity.

\textbf{Vocabulary screen.} We trained skip-gram Word2Vec \cite{mikolov2013word2vec} on all 132,744 cleaned reviews, comprising 608,802 sentences and a vocabulary of 13,380 tokens. The model used 100 dimensions, a window of 5, a minimum count of 5, 5 negative samples and 5 epochs. For seeds present in the trained vocabulary, we queried ten neighbors per authenticity seed and twenty per recipe-attribute seed. The seed lists contained 88 authenticity terms and six attribute groups. Manual curation added 10 authenticity terms and 187 attribute terms (Table~\ref{tab:w2v_lexicon}). A review entered the candidate pool if at least one sentence contained both an authenticity term and a recipe attribute.

\textbf{Clause-level classification.} We split candidate reviews on \texttt{. ! ? ;} and \emph{but}. Four KWIC rules draw on Le et al.'s conformity, deviation and connection cues, with an additional rule for suggested recipe edits. Rule~1, \emph{conformity}, matches \emph{authentic*}, \emph{traditional*}, \emph{genuine}, \emph{real thing} and \emph{real deal}, as in ``authentic Mexican flavor.'' Rule~2, \emph{deviation}, blocks a cue when negation occurs within the preceding seven words, as in ``not authentic.'' Rule~3, \emph{connection}, matches resemblance phrases such as \emph{just like}, \emph{reminds me of} and \emph{close to the original}, provided the clause contains no negation. Rule~4, \emph{recipe edits}, blocks conformity cues in clauses containing phrases such as \emph{more authentic}, \emph{substitute}, \emph{instead of} or \emph{next time}, as in ``substitute fresh herbs to make it more authentic.''

A review is labeled as an endorsement if any clause contains a Rule~1 cue unblocked by Rules~2 and~4, or a qualifying Rule~3 resemblance cue. We compared the dictionary and three-, four- and five-rule versions against 100 reviews labeled by one annotator, including 22 endorsements.

\textbf{Recipe comparison.} A recipe is labeled as endorsed if at least one screened review qualifies. We joined these labels with the network scores and retained recipes with a valid pairing score. Quartiles were formed within each cuisine, breaking score ties by row order. We measured associations using Spearman's $\rho$ and shuffled labels within cuisines 2,000 times. The null $z$-score measures distance from the shuffle mean in shuffle standard deviations. The two-sided empirical $p$-value counts shuffled correlations at least as far from that mean as the observed correlation, adding one to both the count and the number of shuffles. We repeated the pairing test for recipes with exactly one screened review.

\textbf{Embedding check.} We encoded the same 7,281 candidate reviews with \texttt{gte-small} (384 dimensions) and trained class-balanced logistic regression ($C=2$) on a separate 100-review training sample. Five-fold stratified cross-validation selected the cutoff maximizing recall while keeping the out-of-fold false-positive rate at or below 10\%. We then fit the model on the full training sample. Recipe-level outputs were any positive review prediction and the maximum review decision score. We repeated the within-cuisine shuffle test for both outputs.
