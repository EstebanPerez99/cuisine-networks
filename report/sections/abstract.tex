Is a recipe called authentic because of the ingredients it uses? Ahn et al.\ defined the authenticity of an ingredient or ingredient pair as how much more common it is in one cuisine than in others. We ask whether this ingredient-based measure matches what people actually call authentic. Using 26,727 Food.com recipes in nine cuisines and their 132,744 reviews, we build a recipe--ingredient network and score each recipe by how characteristic its ingredients are (\emph{what}), its ingredient pairs are (\emph{which pairs}), and how unusually it combines them compared with randomized networks (\emph{how}). Characteristic pairs are mostly explained by characteristic single ingredients ($r=0.85$), but some, such as chili powder and cumin in Mexican recipes, are combined twice as often as their popularity predicts.
