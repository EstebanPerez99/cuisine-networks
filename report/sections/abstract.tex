Is a recipe called authentic because of the ingredients it uses? Ahn et al.\ measured the authenticity of an ingredient by how much more common it is in one cuisine than in others. We ask whether this matches what people call authentic. Using 26,727 Food.com recipes from nine cuisines and 132,744 reviews, we build a recipe--ingredient network and score each recipe by how distinctive its ingredients are (\emph{what}) and by how much more often its pairs occur together than in randomized networks (\emph{how}). We find the reviews that endorse a recipe as authentic with rules validated on 100 hand-labeled reviews. Among 4,275 recipes, the share endorsed rises from 20.0\% to 32.6\% between the lowest and highest quartile of the \emph{how} score ($\rho = 0.091$, shuffle-test $p = 0.0005$) and stays near 25\% for the \emph{what} score. What reviewers call authentic follows how ingredients are combined more than which ones are used.
