# Manual review of cross-cuisine recipe links

Dataset: 26,727 processed Food.com recipes from `EstebanPerez99/cuisine-networks` (base commit `ca8ff65`). The graph and pairwise comparison are generated by `notebooks/03_network_analysis.ipynb`.

## Review method

I selected three prominent pairs for a closer look: Chinese–Japanese and Greek–Middle Eastern have the two highest relative affinities, while French–Italian contributes a large share of cross-cuisine edge weight and offers interpretable examples. These are not the top three pairs by lift. For each pair, I sorted cross-cuisine network edges by Jaccard similarity, required at least three shared retained ingredients, and selected the first 20 edges without reusing a recipe. I inspected names, original ingredient lists, and available descriptions for 12 of these 60 candidates. The 12 annotations are in `recipe_pair_audit_full.csv`; the full 60-candidate list is in `recipe_pair_candidates_full.csv`. This is a qualitative spot check, not an estimate of labeling errors in the dataset.

## Findings

| Pair | Full-data relative affinity | After removing edges with Jaccard ≥ 0.75 | Interpretation from inspected examples |
| --- | ---: | ---: | --- |
| Chinese–Japanese | 8.54× | 8.59× | Several recipes share soy, sesame, ginger, and noodles. The strongest edge includes a Chinese-tagged recipe explicitly titled “Benihana Japanese Fried Rice,” so it is unsuitable as evidence of a culinary bridge. |
| Greek–Middle Eastern | 4.79× | 4.76× | Tzatziki/cacik and Greek/Turkish chopped salads illustrate overlapping regional dishes. One top pair has identical raw ingredient lists, and a Greek-tagged hummus recipe describes itself as Lebanese. |
| French–Italian | 2.73× | 2.73× | Pistou/pesto is a clear illustrative pair. Some other high-similarity edges are generic batters or baking ingredients, which need cautious interpretation. |

The filtered affinities were recomputed with 199 shuffled-label runs, compared with 999 in the primary pair analysis. Their purpose is to check sensitivity to almost identical ingredient lists; they are not a separate significance test. The sample of 8,019 recipes is a subset of the full data and is not an independent replication.

## Examples worth discussing

- **Chinese–Japanese:** recipe 367177, *Noodles in Sesame Soy Sauce*, and recipe 60731, *Japanese Noodle and Cucumber Salad* (Jaccard 0.67). Both include vermicelli, cucumber, green onion, sesame seeds, soy sauce, and sugar. This shows a shared ingredient pattern, not proof of historical influence.
- **Greek–Middle Eastern:** recipe 307493, *Tzatziki – Gyro Sauce*, and recipe 36126, *Turkish Cacik* (0.75). Both use yogurt, cucumber, garlic, and olive oil. This is a clearer example of related regional preparations than the exact-match potato salad pair.
- **French–Italian:** recipe 245270, *Le Pistou – Vegan Pesto*, and recipe 8650, *Basil Pesto* (0.80). Both use basil, pine nuts, garlic, olive oil, and lemon juice. The pistou description explicitly calls it a French equivalent of Italian pesto.

For the paper, present the pair affinities as **relative shares of cross-cuisine recipe links** in this particular ingredient network. Use the examples to explain what a link can mean, including shared dishes, generic ingredient overlap, and noisy cuisine labels. Do not interpret the links alone as evidence that one cuisine influenced another.
