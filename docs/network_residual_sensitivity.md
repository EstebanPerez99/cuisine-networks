# Does the cuisine residual depend on desserts or near-duplicate recipes?

## Design

The cuisine-stratified ingredient randomization found 62.96% observed within-cuisine edge weight versus a 59.27% randomized median. We rebuilt the same 49 randomized networks, using the same seeds, to test whether this 3.69 percentage-point gap survives three exclusions. Every full-network statistic was checked against the earlier run, and all recipe sizes and within-cuisine ingredient frequencies were verified again.

We applied each exclusion to the **observed and every randomized network**:

1. Remove all edges incident to the 1,307 recipes whose titles explicitly identify a dessert, sweet dish, or drink. This conservative title rule does not recognize all such dishes.
2. Remove all edges incident to the 3,062 recipes in original community 76, the mixed community with a strong sweet-ingredient signature. Membership is fixed from the observed network rather than redetected after randomization.
3. Remove edges with Jaccard similarity at least 0.75, a check on nearly identical ingredient lists. This removes 780 of 309,019 observed edges (0.25%).

The graph is **filtered after** applying its original Jaccard and top-15 construction. We do not reconnect remaining nodes or refill their neighbor lists. This makes each result a comparison of the same induced or edge-filtered network rule across observed and randomized ingredient matrices.

## Results

| Included network | Observed within-cuisine share | Randomized median | Central 95% randomization range | Gap |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| All recipes and edges | 62.96% | 59.27% | 58.75–59.78% | +3.69 pp |
| Without named sweet/drink recipes | 65.33% | 59.72% | 59.12–60.22% | +5.61 pp |
| Without original community 76 | 67.73% | 61.22% | 60.63–61.84% | +6.51 pp |
| Without Jaccard ≥0.75 edges | 62.87% | 59.27% | 58.76–59.78% | +3.60 pp |
| Without community 76 or Jaccard ≥0.75 edges | 67.63% | 61.22% | 60.64–61.85% | +6.41 pp |

No randomized network reached the observed within-cuisine share under any of the five conditions (minimum attainable one-sided Monte Carlo $p=0.02$ with 49 replicates). The residual therefore does not depend on the explicitly named sweet/drink recipes or on very high-similarity edges. The larger gap after removing community 76 suggests that this mixed group *dilutes* the cuisine structure among the remaining recipes, but the size of that increase is descriptive: community 76 was detected from the same observed graph.

The selected pair patterns also change with exclusions. Greek–Middle Eastern links account for 7.28% of cross-cuisine weight after removing named sweet/drink recipes versus a randomized median of 6.26%, and 7.88% after removing community 76 versus 6.15%. Chinese–Japanese is close to or below the randomized median in both filtered networks (5.02% versus 5.29%, and 5.67% versus 5.75%). French–Italian becomes more prominent after removing community 76 (14.44% versus 12.69%), but this comparison is especially sensitive to the network-derived exclusion and is exploratory. Full pair shares and network sizes for every replicate and condition are in `network_residual_sensitivity_full.csv`.

## Interpretation limits

The title rules cover only explicit names, so this does **not** adjust for all recipe types. Original community 76 is selected using the observed ingredient graph, and the randomized graphs connect those same recipes differently; removing it cannot be treated as an independent hypothesis test or a causal decomposition. A Jaccard threshold of 0.75 flags similar ingredient lists, not every copied or nearly identical recipe. All filters are applied after network construction, so the result is specific to this edge set and top-15 rule. The same 49 randomizations underlie every comparison; their $p$ values are correlated and have coarse resolution. Pair comparisons were chosen from prior observed results and are descriptive.
